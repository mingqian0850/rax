6 Discussion Progress in robot learning has largely been driven by collecting more demonstrations and
training larger models. Our results show that a capable agent, when allowed to explore, continues to
improve with the experience it accumulates, without teleoperation and without updates to its
parameters. Because this form of learning relies on general-purpose agents whose capabilities are
advancing rapidly in other domains, every improvement in those agents translates directly into
improved robot learning. The knowledge acquired by RAX agents takes the form of perception and control programs,
skills, and notes. In this form, knowledge executes at the rate of the robot, can be inspected
and verified, composes into longer behaviors, and transfers across tasks, and we
expect code to play the same role for learning from experience that network parameters play
for learning from data. Since the instructions, skills, and memory structures of RAX are
themselves code and text, a natural next step is to let agents improve the architecture itself
from experience, and to share verified skills across fleets of robots, so that a skill learned
by one robot becomes available to all.
